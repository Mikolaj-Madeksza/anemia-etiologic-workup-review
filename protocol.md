# Etiologic Evaluation of Anemia in Adult Medical Inpatients: A Scoping Review of Routine Clinical Practice

## Aim and review question
To map routine investigation of the causes of established anemia in adult medical inpatients, including tests performed, gaps in evaluation and factors associated with investigation.

**Main question:** How is the cause of established anemia investigated in routine care among adult medical inpatients?

Other studies reporting relevant investigations in real clinical care will be included as contextual evidence when their findings cannot be attributed specifically to adult medical inpatients with established anemia.

## Eligibility: population, concept and context (PCC)
| Element | Include | Exclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Population** | Adults aged ≥18 years with established anemia for primary evidence. Anemia may be present on admission or develop during hospitalization, be incidental or the reason for admission, and be of any severity or subtype. Broader or mixed populations, including mixed ages or uncertain anemia status, may contribute contextual evidence if relevant investigations are reported. | Pediatric-only or pregnancy/obstetric-only populations; studies without relevant anemia or iron-deficiency investigation data. |
| **Concept** | Original data on investigations or decisions to investigate the causes of anemia or iron deficiency in clinical care: tests, diagnostic pathways, decisions to investigate or defer, completeness of evaluation, or associated factors. | Anemia detection or hemoglobin monitoring alone; prevalence, prognosis, treatment or transfusion studies without relevant workup data; clinician-only surveys, hypothetical decisions or proposed pathways without observed clinical use. |
| **Context** | Adult medical inpatient care, including internal medicine, geriatrics and medical specialties, with separately identifiable findings (primary evidence); other or mixed clinical settings, including outpatient care (contextual evidence). | Cohorts restricted to intensive care, surgery/perioperative care, trauma or overt acute hemorrhage. |

### What counts as etiologic evaluation?
Eligible investigations include iron studies, B12/folate testing, reticulocytes, blood films, hemolysis tests, other relevant laboratory tests, specialist assessment and procedures such as endoscopy when part of the anemia workup. Tests obtained with the initial blood count can qualify.

A report must contain at least one extractable finding on **whether or how etiologic evaluation occurred**. Lists of anemia causes or laboratory values alone are insufficient; the availability of a test result does not necessarily show that it was ordered to investigate anemia.

Anemia need not be described as “unexplained.” Acute illness, chronic disease or a suspected cause does not automatically make a study ineligible. Equally, absence of a test will not automatically be judged inappropriate.

## Additional inclusion and exclusion criteria
| Issue | Operational rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Study designs** | Include original observational studies, cohorts, cross-sectional studies, clinical audits and quality-improvement or implementation studies reporting actual evaluation practices. |
| **Routine care versus research testing** | Include routine clinical investigations and implemented care pathways. Exclude research-mandated testing panels used only to assign causes; if both are reported, extract routine-care data separately. |
| **Tests, algorithms and procedures** | Include original data on clinical use, underuse or implementation of etiologic tests, consultations or downstream procedures. Exclude accuracy-only studies, proposed algorithms or AI/model comparisons without relevant practice data, and procedure-yield series in patients already selected for confirmed iron-deficiency anemia unless initial evaluation or investigation decisions are reported. |
| **Specialized populations** | Exclude cohorts restricted to intensive care, surgery/perioperative care, trauma or overt acute hemorrhage. Their presence within an otherwise eligible medical cohort does not exclude the whole study. |
| **Mixed populations** | Primary evidence requires separately identifiable results for adult medical inpatients with established anemia. Mixed-age, anemic/nonanemic or treatment-selected cohorts may contribute contextual evidence if relevant clinical investigations are reported. Identify the actual population and denominator; do not present mixed-cohort rates as anemia-specific or inpatient-specific. |
| **Publication type** | Include full original reports with sufficient methods and results. Exclude abstracts alone, case reports, reviews, guidelines, editorials and commentaries from synthesis; use these for context or citation searching when relevant. |
| **Language, date and geography** | No restrictions. Translate non-English reports when necessary and adjudicate uncertain translations. |

Both evidence groups must report investigations or investigation decisions observed in clinical care. A study with separable primary and contextual results may contribute to both sections but will be counted once as a report. Contextual findings will be summarized separately and not treated as inpatient investigation rates.

## Search and report retrieval
Search PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus and Web of Science Core Collection from inception without language restrictions. Combine anemia, hospital-care and etiologic-testing/practice terms, allowing retrieval of hospital laboratory and mixed-setting reports. Check whether the strategy adequately captures broader outpatient contextual evidence and expand it if necessary. Screen references and forward citations of included studies and relevant reviews.

Pilot strategies against known eligible examples. Save database-specific searches, platforms, dates, record counts and citation-search methods; update the searches before submission.

Seek unavailable full reports through institutional access or author contact and record retrieval attempts. Record any unavailable original report as **not retrieved**, rather than PCC-excluded; a secondary summary will not replace it.

## Screening and data extraction
Deduplicate in Zotero and pilot eligibility criteria and the extraction form on a shared sample.

1. **Human assessment:** AR and AA will divide title/abstract screening, full-text screening and extraction, with one human assessment of each assigned record or report.
2. **Independent AI assessment:** ChatGPT-6.0 will assess the same material using the agreed criteria or form without seeing the human decisions or extractions.
3. **Adjudication and records:** Compare outputs; MM will resolve discrepancies and high-risk decisions, including uncertain eligibility or primary/contextual status, denominators, conflicting data, translations and separation of clinical from research testing. Retain final human-approved decisions, full-text exclusion reasons and adjudication notes. Uncertain title/abstract records will advance to full text; AI alone will not exclude a record.

This is **human–AI verification, not duplicate independent human screening**. Record the actual model/version, dates, prompts and any workflow deviations. Humans remain responsible for source verification and final decisions.

## Data to extract
- Citation, country, design, period, setting and primary/contextual classification.
- Population, sample size, age, anemia definition, severity/subtype and relevant comorbidities.
- Investigations and pathways, definitions, timing and whether testing occurred in routine care.
- Eligible, investigated and non-investigated counts, test-specific numerators/denominators and missing or unclear data.
- Reasons for investigation or non-investigation, associated factors, author-defined adequacy standards and diagnostic findings linked to the workup.
- Study limitations, eligibility or translation uncertainty and overlapping populations or reports.

Do not treat unreported tests as unperformed or assume that a reported laboratory summary means everyone was tested. Distinguish inconclusive workup from absent or insufficient workup, and link reports of the same study to prevent double counting.

## Synthesis and reporting
Summarize primary inpatient and contextual findings in separate tables and narratives: study characteristics, testing patterns, completeness and timing, associated factors and evidence gaps. Preserve study-specific definitions and denominators, and distinguish actual practice from authors’ judgments of appropriateness.

No meta-analysis, formal risk-of-bias assessment or certainty grading is planned. Discuss study limitations without making causal claims or treating contextual evidence as representative of inpatient care.

Follow JBI scoping-review guidance and report according to PRISMA-ScR, including the human–AI workflow, selection flow diagram, full-text exclusion reasons and separate primary/contextual counts.
